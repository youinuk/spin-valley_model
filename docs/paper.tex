\title{Model-form versus finite-sampling uncertainty in
micromagnet-assisted spin--valley shuttling: a periodic-reference
sensitivity atlas}

\author{Inuk You}
\email{youinuk@gmail.com}
\affiliation{Department of Science, Kyungdong High School, Seoul, Republic of Korea}

\date{\today}

\begin{abstract}
Coherent electron spin shuttling in Si/SiGe couples spin to the valley degree
of freedom, so design studies of micromagnet geometry must assume a local
spin--valley coupling profile $\lambda_{sv}(x)$ that is not independently
fixed. We ask how far design-facing conclusions depend on that assumption, and
how that dependence compares with the variation a finite Monte-Carlo ensemble
produces on its own. Using a periodic micromagnet array as a controlled
reference geometry, we evaluate a 108-condition grid under four diagnostic
coupling ans\"atze. Two comparisons are placed on one scale: the
\emph{cross-ansatz} difference, obtained by changing the coupling model on a
fixed set of noise realizations, and the \emph{cross-seed} difference, obtained
by changing the noise sample under a fixed coupling model. Disagreement is the fraction of conditions the two members
of a pair assign to different nine-category labels, formed by classifying each
of the two response channels as improve, inactive, or worsen. Cross-ansatz disagreement is
$0.394$ at the pre-registered endpoint of $n=30$ realizations and $0.396$ at
the registered follow-up endpoint of $n=100$, and stays within a two-point band
across all eight checkpoints, while same-ansatz cross-seed disagreement falls
monotonically from $0.540$ to $0.302$. At the registered checkpoints through $n=30$ the sampling disagreement is
comparable to or larger than the cross-ansatz disagreement; from $n=40$ to
$100$ the ordering is reversed. The balance is observable dependent: resolving the
label into its two response channels, the cross-ansatz to cross-seed ratio at
$n=100$ is $4.75$ for spin dephasing and $0.94$ for valley excitation, a
post-hoc decomposition. A post-hoc ranking diagnostic shows that rank correlation
across ans\"atze does not track the classification: two ansatz pairs with
nearly identical rank correlations differ by 33 percentage points in
classification agreement. Operating points selected for stability across noise
samples alone remain robust under all four coupling profiles in some cases and
under none in others. Design conclusions obtained under one assumed
coupling profile therefore remain model-conditional until the effective
coupling is independently constrained, or robustness is demonstrated across a
justified model family.
\end{abstract}

Coherent transport of electron spin qubits between distant sites
(``shuttling'') lets qubit registers sit apart from readout and control
hardware, and is a key enabler for scalable Si/SiGe
architectures~\cite{kunne_spinbus_2024,langrock_blueprint_2023,gonzalez-zalba_scaling_2021,veldhorst_silicon_2017,li_crossbar_2018,vandersypen_interfacing_2017}.
The platform is mature: high-fidelity gates, multi-qubit processors, error
correction, established single-spin readout and an industrial fabrication path
are all in
place~\cite{xue_quantum_2022,tanttu_assessment_2024,huang_high-fidelity_2024,philips_universal_2022,fernandez_de_fuentes_running_2026,thorvaldson_grovers_2025,takeda_quantum_2022,meunier_silicon_2025,koch_industrial_2025,elsayed_low_2024,hanson_spins_2007,burkard_semiconductor_2023,morello_single-shot_2010,hu_single-electron_2025}.
Conveyor-mode and bucket-brigade shuttling have been demonstrated at high
fidelity~\cite{de_smet_high-fidelity_2025,seidler_conveyor-mode_2022,mills_shuttling_2019},
as have shuttling-based two-qubit links between separated
registers~\cite{noiri_shuttling-based_2022,matsumoto_two-qubit_2025,ademi_distributing_2025},
and gates acting on moving spins have been proposed
theoretically~\cite{fernandez-fernandez_spin-orbit-enabled_2025}.
A central obstacle is the valley degree of freedom.
The two low-lying conduction-band valleys in strained Si are nearly
degenerate, so a shuttled electron can leak into the excited valley. Spatial
variation of the valley energies along the path therefore couples motion to the
valley sector.
Experimentally, low-$E_{\rm VS}$
pockets arising from alloy disorder are a known threat to shuttling
fidelity~\cite{paquelet_wuetz_atomic_2022,losert_strategies_2024,zwerver_shuttling_2023},
and recent shuttling-based maps report physical valley splittings
$E_{\rm VS}$ from
$1.5$ to $200\,\mu$eV along real conveyor
trajectories, dominated by alloy disorder, with trajectory selection
around low-splitting regions demonstrated as a practical
mitigation~\cite{volmer_impact_2026}. When combined with a micromagnet-induced synthetic
spin--orbit field~\cite{unseld_baseband_2025,yoneda_quantum-dot_2018},
valley transitions become correlated with spin
dephasing, so that geometry and trajectory choices affect spin
coherence and valley leakage simultaneously.

A natural program is \emph{valley-aware co-design}: jointly optimizing
micromagnet geometry and shuttling trajectory to suppress both valley leakage
and dephasing. Any such optimization assumes a model of how the two sectors
couple---an effective spin--valley Hamiltonian $H_{sv}$. The literature
motivates several ingredients, among them gradient-induced synthetic
spin--orbit coupling, valley-pocket localization, and spin--valley hot spots,
but leaves the local profile $\lambda_{sv}(x)$ for a shuttled dot open.

We ask a prior question: \emph{how much do co-design conclusions depend on that
choice, and how does that dependence compare with the variation a finite
Monte-Carlo ensemble produces on its own?} The comparison is the point. Showing
that four coupling profiles give four answers means little if the same
simulation gives different answers to itself.

We therefore fix the geometry, the operator structure, the noise model and the
condition grid, and drive all four ans\"atze with common random numbers, so
that every cross-ansatz comparison is made on one realization set. Against it we
place the disagreement measured between independent seed blocks under a fixed
ansatz. Both are counted on the same design-facing classification: whether each
of the two coupling-induced responses is negative, positive, or below its
operational floor, against floors fixed in advance. We repeat the
comparison at Monte-Carlo ensemble sizes from $n_{\rm real}=5$ to $100$, where
$n_{\rm real}$ is the number of independent charge-noise realizations averaged
per condition. Figure~\ref{fig:overview} summarizes the setup, the four
ans\"atze, the experiment, and the matched comparison.

The estimands, the candidate-selection rule and the checkpoints
$n_{\rm real}=5,10,20,30$ with endpoint $n_{\rm real}=30$ were fixed in writing
before any of these data existed. After the $n_{\rm real}=30$ result showed
cross-seed disagreement still falling, we registered a follow-up extension to
$n_{\rm real}=100$, with checkpoints, hard stop and reporting language fixed
before the extension data were generated. Diagnostics added after seeing results are marked post-hoc
where they appear.

An earlier archived calculation of this atlas drew a separate noise realization
for each ansatz, so a cross-ansatz difference there also carried a sampling
difference. We therefore begin by quantifying how much that confounded the
apparent model disagreement, before turning to the matched comparison.

The main findings are as follows. Cross-ansatz
disagreement --- the fraction of conditions two ans\"atze label differently ---
is $0.396$ at $n_{\rm real}=100$ and stays within a two-point band across all
eight checkpoints, while cross-seed disagreement falls monotonically from
$0.540$ to $0.302$; at
the registered checkpoints through $n_{\rm real}=30$ the sampling disagreement
is comparable to or larger than the cross-ansatz disagreement, and at the
checkpoints from $40$ to $100$ the ordering is reversed. The balance is observable dependent: resolving the label
into its two response channels, the cross-ansatz to cross-seed ratio at
$n_{\rm real}=100$ is $4.75$ for spin dephasing and $0.94$ for valley
excitation, a post-hoc decomposition. A post-hoc ranking diagnostic shows that rank
correlation across ans\"atze does not track the classification: two ansatz
pairs whose rank correlations differ by $0.003$ differ by 33 percentage points
in classification agreement. Operating points selected for stability across noise samples
alone remain robust under all four coupling profiles in some cases and under
none in others.

\section{Related work and positioning}

Prior studies of valley physics in Si/SiGe have characterized valley
splitting statistics~\cite{paquelet_wuetz_atomic_2022,losert_practical_2023},
the role of interface disorder and valley--orbit
coupling~\cite{friesen_theory_2010,hosseinkhani_theory_2022}, and
shuttling-induced valley
excitation~\cite{langrock_blueprint_2023,losert_strategies_2024,volmer_mapping_2024,oda_suppressing_2026}. Work on micromagnet-based
spin manipulation~\cite{unseld_baseband_2025,yoneda_quantum-dot_2018} has
established synthetic
spin--orbit coupling as a control resource, and the role of
spin--orbit interaction and $g$-tensor anisotropy in quantum dots has
been studied in both electron and hole
systems~\cite{tanttu_controlling_2019,jin_probing_2024,geyer_anisotropic_2024,hendrickx_sweet-spot_2024}.
Charge-noise dephasing
during shuttling and in static dots has been modeled and measured with
filter-function and disorder-aware
approaches~\cite{langrock_blueprint_2023,struck_low-frequency_2020,krzywda_coherence_2026,burkard_semiconductor_2023,yoneda_noise-correlation_2023},
and hopping/baseband control with site-dependent quantization axes has
emerged as an alternative to resonant
driving~\cite{wang_operating_2024,unseld_baseband_2025}. Disorder and
strain effects on spin--orbit coupling have been quantified in related
Ge hole-spin device models~\cite{sarkar_effect_2025,mauro_strain_2025}.
Trajectory and device parameters for shuttling \emph{have} been
optimized in specific studies, including velocity and design trade-offs
in blueprint analyses and disorder-aware shuttling
strategies~\cite{langrock_blueprint_2023,losert_strategies_2024}.
Generic optimal-control machinery is also
available~\cite{khaneja_optimal_2005,niu_universal_2019}.

In such studies the effective coupling model is held fixed, which is the
right choice where the model is validated for the regime in question. The
setting we address is the local spin--valley coupling of a dot shuttled
through a micromagnet field. No generally validated local
$\lambda_{sv}(x)$ that can be adopted without additional modeling
assumptions is yet available for this setting (Sec.~\ref{sec:model}), so there the
model choice itself becomes a source of design risk. Quantifying that risk is the purpose of this work. Recent conveyor-mode
measurements increasingly constrain the relevant microscopic
ingredients. They provide spatial maps of the valley splitting $E_{\rm VS}$ along
shuttling trajectories and, together with valley-resolved $g$-factor
maps, constrain the complex intervalley
coupling~\cite{volmer_impact_2026,volmer_gfactor_2026}. These
measurements do not by themselves determine the effective local
moving-dot spin--valley kernel parameterized here. They sharpen the
experimental target that a future microscopic $\lambda_{sv}(x)$ must
fit. Valley-aware
co-design in the sense used here means jointly tuning geometry and
trajectory against both valley leakage and dephasing. We accordingly treat it as a prospective program.

Our contribution is methodological. We fix the four-level response model, the reference
geometry and the condition grid, and vary two things against each other: the
coupling ansatz, under common random numbers, and the seed block, at fixed
ansatz. Both are tracked across ensemble size. Each ansatz is a kernel
multiplying one fixed minimal spin--valley operator. Micromagnets and electron shuttling are individually
established experimentally~\cite{yoneda_quantum-dot_2018,unseld_baseband_2025,de_smet_high-fidelity_2025,seidler_conveyor-mode_2022},
but periodically patterned micromagnet shuttling architectures remain
largely proposed or emerging
designs~\cite{kunne_spinbus_2024,langrock_blueprint_2023,unseld_baseband_2025}; we
accordingly treat the periodic array as a controlled reference geometry
for the diagnostic comparison. The design conclusions drawn on that geometry remain
conditional on the assumed coupling profile. They can be strengthened by
independently constraining that profile, or by demonstrating robustness
across a justified model family.

\section{Model}
\label{sec:model}

One part of the simulator varies in this study: the spatial form of the
spin--valley coupling, $\lambda_{sv}(x)$. The geometry, the noise model, the
operator structure and every other model ingredient are held fixed. The
sections below set up that fixed backdrop and then give the four alternative
forms of $\lambda_{sv}(x)$.

We consider an electron shuttled at constant velocity $v$ along $x$
through a periodic micromagnet array, with a position-dependent
diabatic valley detuning
%
\begin{equation}
\varepsilon_v(x)=\varepsilon_{v,\rm base}-\left(\varepsilon_{v,\rm base}-\varepsilon_{v,\min}\right)
\exp\!\left[-\frac{(x-x_c)^2}{2\sigma_p^2}\right],
\label{eq:evx}
\end{equation}
%
a baseline with a Gaussian pocket (minimum) near the shuttling path, using
$\varepsilon_{v,\rm base}=100\,\mu$eV, $\varepsilon_{v,\min}=5\,\mu$eV and
$\sigma_p=30$\,nm; for A$_{\rm pocket}$ we set
$\sigma_\lambda=\sigma_p$. The constant-velocity trajectory spans
$x\in[x_c-4\sigma_p,\,x_c+4\sigma_p]$, a total path length of
$240$\,nm. This detuning landscape is a phenomenological surrogate for
the measured spatial variation of the physical valley splitting
$E_{\rm VS}$. That variation is expected from alloy-disorder and interface
physics~\cite{langrock_blueprint_2023,paquelet_wuetz_atomic_2022,losert_practical_2023},
and has been mapped directly along a shuttling path by conveyor-mode
spin-coherent measurements~\cite{volmer_mapping_2024}. Representative values for the
device geometry, valley energetics, and shuttling velocity are
summarized in Table~\ref{tab:params}.

We model the spin and valley degrees of freedom with a minimal
four-level effective Hamiltonian
%
\begin{align}
H(x) = \tfrac{1}{2}g\mu_B [B_{\rm ext}+B_z(x)]\,\sigma_z\otimes I_v
      + \tfrac{1}{2}g\mu_B B_x(x)\,\sigma_x\otimes I_v \nonumber\\
      + \tfrac{1}{2}\varepsilon_v(x)\,I_s\otimes\tau_z
      + \Delta_v\,I_s\otimes\tau_x
      + \lambda_{sv}(x)\,\sigma_x\otimes\tau_x,
\end{align}
%
where $\sigma_i$ and $\tau_i$ are Pauli operators on the spin and
valley two-level spaces, $B_z(x)$ and $B_x(x)$ are the longitudinal and
transverse components of the \emph{micromagnet stray field}, and
$B_{\rm ext}=0.5$\,T is the uniform external field along $z$. The spin
block is the Zeeman Hamiltonian in the total longitudinal field
$B_{\rm ext}+B_z(x)$ together with the micromagnet transverse field; the
transverse term $g\mu_B B_x(x)$ is a
position-dependent transverse Zeeman \emph{energy}, not an AC EDSR
drive. Because $B_{\rm ext}$ is spatially uniform it drops out of every
spatial derivative, so the gradient-based ans\"atze below use the
micromagnet stray-field gradients $\partial_x B_z(x)$ and
$\partial_x B_x(x)$. The valley block is a generic two-level avoided-crossing model
with diabatic detuning $\varepsilon_v(x)$ and off-diagonal coupling
$\Delta_v$, so its isolated eigenlevel splitting is
$E_{\rm gap}(x)=\sqrt{\varepsilon_v(x)^2+4\Delta_v^2}$. Over the
$5$--$100\,\mu$eV input range used here this exceeds $\varepsilon_v(x)$
by less than $2\%$, with the largest relative difference at the pocket
minimum and less than $0.02\%$ near the $\varepsilon_v\simeq E_Z$ crossings that
set the proxy hot spots.

Three valley energies appear here: the diabatic input detuning
$\varepsilon_v(x)$, the valley-block eigenlevel splitting $E_{\rm gap}(x)$, and
the physical valley splitting $E_{\rm VS}$ reported by experiment. Of the two
model quantities, $E_{\rm gap}$ is the analogue of a measured $E_{\rm VS}$. The
measured $E_{\rm VS}$ range motivates the scale chosen for $\varepsilon_v$; for
$\Delta_v=0.5\,\mu$eV, $E_{\rm gap}$ and $\varepsilon_v$ agree to within $2\%$
over that range. $\Delta_v$ is a
phenomenological mixing parameter; microscopically such intervalley coupling
arises from interface and valley--orbit physics~\cite{friesen_theory_2010}.

This Hamiltonian is a diagnostic effective model, not a microscopically derived
Si/SiGe $H_{sv}$. The term $\lambda_{sv}\sigma_x\otimes\tau_x$ is a minimal
real spin-flip/valley-flip Pauli product, one of several possible structures
(e.g.\ $\sigma_x\tau_y$ or $\sigma_y\tau_x$). Microscopically, spin--valley
mixing follows from matrix elements of intrinsic and synthetic spin--orbit
terms between valley-dependent envelope
states~\cite{hosseinkhani_theory_2022,huang_spin_2014}. All four ans\"atze
share this one operator structure and differ only in the scalar profile
$\lambda_{sv}(x)$: four spatial kernels on one fixed operator, not four
microscopic Hamiltonians. That scope holds for every statement about the
ans\"atze below.

The coupling strength is set by a profile $\lambda_{sv}(x)$ whose form is
itself uncertain, so we compare four toy-level ans\"atze. Each is motivated by
a known ingredient without being derived from it: spatial Zeeman-energy
variation from the micromagnet
gradients~\cite{langrock_blueprint_2023,struck_low-frequency_2020};
transverse-gradient synthetic spin--orbit
coupling~\cite{borjans_single-spin_2019,zhang_controlling_2021,hosseinkhani_theory_2022};
valley-pocket
localization~\cite{paquelet_wuetz_atomic_2022,losert_strategies_2024,volmer_mapping_2024};
and the spin--valley hot spot at near-degeneracy of the dressed spin and valley
sectors~\cite{yang_spin-valley_2013,huang_spin_2014}, which the released
kernels represent by the input-detuning proxy
$\varepsilon_v(x)\simeq E_Z(x)$. We use
the physically transparent total-field convention throughout, in which
the Zeeman energy is evaluated with the full local field,
%
\begin{equation}
E_Z(x) = g\mu_B\,[\,B_{\rm ext} + B_z(x)\,],
\label{eq:ez}
\end{equation}
%
with $B_{\rm ext}=0.5$\,T (Table~\ref{tab:params}). The four ans\"atze
are then
%
\begin{align}
& \text{A:} \quad \lambda_{sv}(x) = \lambda_0\,
  \frac{|\partial_x B_z(x)|}{\max_x|\partial_x B_z|}, \\
& \text{A}_{\rm pocket}\text{:} \quad \lambda_{sv}(x) = \lambda_{sv}^{\rm A}(x)\,
  \exp\!\left[-\frac{(x-x_c)^2}{2\sigma_\lambda^2}\right], \\
& \text{B}_z\text{:} \quad \lambda_{sv}(x) = \lambda_{sv}^{\rm A}(x)\,
  \exp\!\left[-\frac{(\varepsilon_v(x)-E_Z(x))^2}{2\sigma_E^2}\right], \\
& \text{B}_x\text{:} \quad \lambda_{sv}(x) = \lambda_0\,
  \frac{|\partial_x B_x(x)|}{\max_x|\partial_x B_x|}\,
  \exp\!\left[-\frac{(\varepsilon_v(x)-E_Z(x))^2}{2\sigma_E^2}\right],
\end{align}
%
where $E_Z(x)$ is the local Zeeman energy of Eq.~\eqref{eq:ez}, so that
the diagnostic condition $\varepsilon_v(x)\simeq E_Z(x)$ produces two flank proxy
crossings
around the pocket. (Under an alternative bookkeeping convention, the window is evaluated
with a stray-field-only Zeeman energy, which shifts the resonance
windows toward the pocket bottom and, as we show in
Sec.~\ref{sec:results}, moves the atlas statistics substantially. This
convention sensitivity is itself part of the model risk we report.)

A, the \emph{longitudinal-gradient} profile, is the baseline, motivated by the
spatial Zeeman-energy variation the micromagnet imprints. A$_{\rm pocket}$, the
\emph{pocket-localized gradient}, weights that same gradient coupling by a
Gaussian envelope centred on the low-$\varepsilon_v$ pocket. B$_z$ and B$_x$, the \emph{longitudinal-} and
\emph{transverse-gradient hot-spot} profiles, are resonance-window ans\"atze
motivated by the spin--valley
hot spot~\cite{yang_spin-valley_2013,huang_spin_2014}; their
Gaussian windows are centred on the input-detuning condition
$\varepsilon_v(x)\simeq E_Z(x)$ and act as proxies for the dressed crossing,
with no claim to a microscopic line shape. They use two alternative choices
of
magnetic-gradient component: the longitudinal gradient $\partial_x B_z$
sets the local Zeeman-splitting variation (the dephasing
gradient~\cite{langrock_blueprint_2023,struck_low-frequency_2020}), whereas the
transverse gradient $\partial_x B_x$ is the component that drives spin
flips in slanting-field
EDSR~\cite{tokura_coherent_2006,pioro-ladriere_electrically_2008,zhang_controlling_2021}. Neither
choice is prescribed for a shuttled dot by the literature, which is
precisely the model uncertainty probed here.

None of the four profiles should be
interpreted as a literature-standard or microscopically derived
spin--valley coupling; the Gaussian resonance window
$e^{-(\varepsilon_v-E_Z)^2/(2\sigma_E^2)}$ in particular is a regularized
proxy for the hot-spot enhancement, not a standard
expression. These kernels quantify how strongly the
conclusions depend on plausible, non-unique modelling choices.

One might ask whether the gradient-only kernels A and
A$_{\rm pocket}$ are physically admissible, given the known
spin--valley enhancement near $\varepsilon_v\approx E_Z$. In the diabatic
basis of the Hamiltonian above, the operator
$\lambda_{sv}(x)\,\sigma_x\otimes\tau_x$ couples a pair of states whose
uncoupled energy difference is $\varepsilon_v(x)-E_Z(x)$, taken in the diagonal
part of the diabatic Hamiltonian before $\Delta_v$, $B_x$ and $\lambda_{sv}$
are diagonalized. The dressed condition is $E_{\rm gap}\simeq E_{\rm spin}$, with
$E_{\rm spin}(x)=g\mu_B\sqrt{[B_{\rm ext}+B_z(x)]^2+B_x(x)^2}$; we use the
diabatic one as a proxy for it, hence ``proxy crossings'' throughout. A nonzero
off-diagonal coupling at a flank crossing therefore produces an
avoided crossing and resonantly enhanced spin--valley mixing through
the four-level dynamics, even when $\lambda_{sv}(x)$ contains no
explicit resonance window~\cite{yang_spin-valley_2013,huang_spin_2014}.

For the profiles considered here, A and A$_{\rm pocket}$ remain
nonzero at the relevant flank crossings. The window in B$_z$/B$_x$
tests the distinct, stronger phenomenological assumption that the
effective off-diagonal coupling amplitude is itself localized near
resonance, thereby adding profile localization to the dynamical
level-crossing enhancement. Microscopic spin--valley matrix elements
depend on spin--orbit and field-gradient matrix elements between
valley-dependent envelope states and need not take the particular
Gaussian form in $\varepsilon_v-E_Z$ used
here~\cite{hosseinkhani_theory_2022,huang_spin_2014}. The A-type
kernels should therefore be read as diagnostic detuning-independent
profiles, not as uniquely derived microscopic matrix elements. Neither
family is a null straw man; together they bracket complementary
assumptions about the spatial structure of the same off-diagonal
coupling.

Figure~\ref{fig:profiles} shows the four profiles for a representative
pocket: the gradient-only model A is delocalized over the whole
period, A$_{\rm pocket}$ concentrates the gradient coupling near the
pocket, and the resonance models B$_z$/B$_x$ are sharply peaked where
$\varepsilon_v(x)$ crosses $E_Z(x)$. An important subtlety arises because
all three of A$_{\rm pocket}$, B$_z$, and B$_x$ \emph{multiply the
field gradient} $|\partial_x B_z|$ (or $|\partial_x B_x|$). The two gradient components are set by the array
alone, not by the pocket. By symmetry $B_z$ is extremal and $B_x$
vanishes at the array-symmetry point $x=0$, so $|\partial_x B_z|$ has a
node there; for the fitted baseline field $|\partial_x B_x|$ also attains
its maximum at that point. The centred-pocket configuration places
$x_c=0$ there as well, so the node--pocket alignment follows from that
placement; it is not a generic property of a centred valley pocket.
Consequently A$_{\rm pocket}=|\partial_x B_z|\times$Gaussian is \emph{zero at
the pocket centre} and forms two symmetric peaks on the flanks. A$_{\rm pocket}$ is thus
``pocket-localized'' in its Gaussian envelope but inherits the
gradient's flank structure; only when the pocket sits off-centre (where
the gradient no longer vanishes at the well bottom) does its weight
become strongly asymmetric and single-flank dominated. The resonance
windows (B$_z$, B$_x$)
activate symmetrically on both flanks for the centred pocket as well;
the profiles therefore differ not in which side of the pocket they
occupy but in their \emph{shape, width, phase relative to the driving
gradient, and amplitude}. B$_x$ is built from $\partial_x B_x$ in place of
$\partial_x B_z$, and the two gradient components are approximately phase
shifted. Its weight therefore peaks slightly outside the crossing, where the
$\partial_x B_z$-based kernels peak slightly inside it. This offset is
consistent with the distinct thresholded-classification behaviour B$_x$ shows
in the atlas. A left/right asymmetry appears only for an off-centred pocket,
where the two flank crossings are inequivalent. The centred-pocket profiles
used here are two-sided and symmetric, so any single-sided ``opposite side''
reading is an argmax tie-break artifact of exactly degenerate twin peaks.

$\Delta_v$ and $\sigma_E$ are toy parameters of the model, not measured
constants. The geometric and velocity baselines are representative of reported
Si/SiGe shuttling devices.

The observables target the two loss channels that the literature
identifies as central to Si/SiGe shuttling: valley excitation
(leakage) and valley-induced spin
dephasing~\cite{losert_strategies_2024,de_smet_high-fidelity_2025,volmer_mapping_2024,oda_suppressing_2026}.
We quantify them as the change in valley-leakage probability
$\Delta P_v$ and the change in dephasing phase variance
$\Delta\chi_\phi$ induced by the spin--valley coupling. The two ask different questions: $\Delta P_v$ asks how much charge
noise changes valley leakage, and $\Delta\chi_\phi$ asks how much the valley
sector changes charge-noise dephasing. They are two cross-effects, not two
components of one total error, and each therefore needs its own reference. We
use three nested models: a spin-only reference with charge noise but no valley
sector ($\mathrm{M1}$), a valley-aware reference without charge noise
($\mathrm{M1V}$), and the full model with both charge noise and valley coupling
($\mathrm{M2}$). The two responses use \emph{different} references by
design, each separating one cross-effect:
$\Delta P_v = P_v^{\mathrm{M2}}-P_v^{\mathrm{M1V}}$ is the effect of charge
noise on valley leakage, and
$\Delta\chi_\phi = \chi^{\mathrm{M2}}-\chi^{\mathrm{M1}}$ the effect of the
valley sector on charge-noise dephasing. The dephasing is computed within a
filter-function description of $1/f$ charge
noise~\cite{burkard_semiconductor_2023,struck_low-frequency_2020,viola_dynamical_1999}.
Thus $\mathcal{R} = (\Delta P_v, \Delta\chi_\phi)$ is not a
decomposition of a single infidelity but a pair of cross-effect
measures. $\Delta P_v$ and $\Delta\chi_\phi$ are
\emph{baseline-relative response diagnostics}, not absolute error
rates. Absolute performance would be read from the full-model values
$P_v^{\mathrm{M2}}$ and $\chi_\phi^{\mathrm{M2}}$ (or a derived
phase-error proxy $p_\phi=(1-e^{-\chi_\phi/2})/2$), which we do not
optimize here.

Under the standard assumption that each component is
monotonic in the infidelity of its channel (larger leakage and larger
dephasing both reduce fidelity), we classify each condition by the
sign of each \emph{above-threshold} response. The effect-size floors are
$|\Delta P_v|\ge 10^{-4}$ and $|\Delta\chi_\phi|\ge 10^{-3}$. A condition
clearing both is \emph{robust} if both improve
($\Delta P_v<0$ and $\Delta\chi_\phi<0$), \emph{valley-trade} or
\emph{spin-trade} if the signs oppose, and \emph{both-worsen} if both worsen.
The realization count $n_{\rm real}$ introduced above counts Monte Carlo
repetitions of the simulated noise, not experimental repetitions, and is
the column name used in the released data tables and scripts. These floors are operational criteria that set a
below-threshold zone on channel-appropriate response scales. They come from
neither an external fidelity standard nor a statistical-significance
calculation, and the decade offset between $10^{-4}$ and $10^{-3}$ follows the
decade separating the natural scales of the two channels. Sensitivity to shared below-threshold assignments is examined in the
Supplementary Material, and the stability of individual classifications is
tested by the seed-stable candidate screen of Sec.~\ref{sec:results}. When only one channel is above threshold, the
condition takes a separate single-channel category, which keeps one-channel
cases out of the \emph{both-worsen} class. The classification does not compare the two channels'
magnitudes against each other: each channel is judged against its own floor,
and the category then uses the sign of any above-threshold response. The rank
correlation instead uses the response magnitude
$|\mathcal{R}|=\sqrt{\Delta P_v^2+\Delta\chi_\phi^2}$, whose ordering
is preserved under uniform rescaling but does depend on the relative
weighting of the two channels (we report this dependence as a
robustness check).

A co-design decision reads off the
direction of the channel responses (whether each channel improves or
worsens), not only their magnitude. We therefore treat the four
two-channel quadrants as the decision-facing sign diagnostic and the ranking as
a complementary magnitude-ordering summary. Both remain informative; they
answer different questions and carry different degrees of model dependence.

\section{Methods}
\label{sec:methods}
\subsection{Condition grid and reference geometry}

We use two pocket placements: $x_c=0$ for the centred case, aligned with the
array-symmetry point, and $x_c=25$\,nm for the off-centred edge case. On top of
each we scan nine geometries -- the baseline plus one additive $+$ and $-$ step
in each of four parameters, \texttt{period} ($\pm30$\,nm), \texttt{depth}
($\pm10$\,nm), \texttt{half\_x} ($\pm5$\,nm) and \texttt{half\_z} ($\pm3$\,nm),
i.e.\ $1+4\times2=9$ -- together with three velocities and two coupling
strengths $\lambda$. Here \texttt{depth} is the signed vertical coordinate of
the quantum well relative to the magnet plane, $-50$\,nm at baseline; a $+$
step moves the well toward the magnet array, reducing the magnet--well
separation and strengthening the stray field. The grid holds
$2\times9\times3\times2=108$ conditions, each evaluated under all four
ans\"atze, giving 432 ansatz-condition cells. The two coupling strengths are
$\lambda=0.5$ and $1.0\,\mu$eV, so the grid contains six
$(v,\lambda)$ families.

Each condition yields the response pair
$\mathcal{R}=(\Delta P_v,\Delta\chi_\phi)$ of Sec.~\ref{sec:model} and, from
it, a thresholded label drawn from nine categories: the four two-channel
quadrants (\emph{robust}, \emph{valley-trade}, \emph{spin-trade},
\emph{both-worsen}), four single-channel categories, and
\emph{below-threshold}. We reserve ``quadrant'' for the four two-channel
labels and call exact agreement of the nine-category label
\emph{classification agreement}. The label is an exact bijection of the two
channels' three-state descriptions (improve, inactive, worsen), a property the
channel-resolved diagnostics below rely on. The effect-size floors
$|\Delta P_v|\ge10^{-4}$ and $|\Delta\chi_\phi|\ge10^{-3}$ are operational
criteria, not significance cutoffs or Monte-Carlo confidence bounds.

\subsection{Matched cross-ansatz and cross-seed comparison}

The primary experiment measures how far the design-facing label depends on the
assumed coupling profile, against how far it moves under a change of random
sample alone. We evaluate the full grid in five seed blocks. Within a block,
all four ans\"atze are driven by common random numbers, so every cross-ansatz
comparison is made on one realization set; across blocks the realization seeds
are disjoint. Each block is evaluated at nested realization counts
$n_{\rm real}=5,10,20,30,40,60,80,100$, so the smaller counts are prefixes of
the larger and the checkpoints lie on one trajectory, not on independent runs.

Two estimands are defined on the nine-category label $L_{acb}$ of condition
$c\in\mathcal{C}$ under ansatz $a\in\mathcal{A}$ in block $b\in\mathcal{B}$,
with $|\mathcal{C}|=108$, $|\mathcal{A}|=4$ and $|\mathcal{B}|=5$. For each
ansatz pair and each block pair,
%
\begin{align}
d_{aa'} &= \big\langle \mathbf{1}[L_{acb}\neq L_{a'cb}] \big\rangle_{c,b},
\nonumber\\
d_{bb'} &= \big\langle \mathbf{1}[L_{acb}\neq L_{acb'}] \big\rangle_{c,a},
\label{eq:pairwise-d}
\end{align}
%
each a plain mean over the cells in which both members of the pair exist. The
two estimands are the uniform averages over pairs,
%
\begin{equation}
D_{\rm ansatz} = \frac{1}{6}\!\!\sum_{a<a'}\! d_{aa'},
\qquad
D_{\rm seed} = \frac{1}{10}\!\!\sum_{b<b'}\! d_{bb'} .
\label{eq:D}
\end{equation}
%
Both are reported with all sixteen pairwise values, together with the
difference
$\Delta_D=D_{\rm ansatz}-D_{\rm seed}$ and the ratio
$R_D=D_{\rm ansatz}/D_{\rm seed}$. Complete separation of the two pairwise
distributions would be sufficient for uniform dominance; it is reported as an
observation, not used as a gate, and no numerical interpretation threshold is
applied.

$D_{\rm ansatz}$ is itself computed from finite samples and retains an
ansatz$\times$realization interaction, so it is not a pure model-form
component. $D_{\rm seed}$ is a finite-sampling label-instability benchmark, not
an estimator variance: the classifier is a threshold nonlinearity, and the two
quantities are compared, not decomposed. $D_{\rm ansatz}+D_{\rm seed}$ is not a
decomposition of anything.

A shared \emph{below-threshold} assignment counts as agreement, so the
dilution is reported, not conditioned away. Three quantities carry it:
$p_{\rm all}$, the fraction of conditions inactive under every ansatz and
block; the below-threshold rates $B_{\rm ansatz}$ and $B_{\rm seed}$; and the
share of agreement contributed by shared inactivity. A union-conditioned recomputation,
restricted to comparisons in which at least one member of the pair carries a
two-channel quadrant label, is reported as a sensitivity analysis only. Intersection conditioning is not
used.

\subsection{Channel-resolved diagnostics}

The two responses are isolated against different references by construction, so
they carry different baselines and are never pooled. We therefore repeat the
comparison on each channel's three-state description, which satisfies the
exact identity
\begin{equation}
D_{\rm joint} = D_{P} + D_{\chi} - D_{\rm both}
\label{eq:channel-identity}
\end{equation}
for both the ansatz and the seed averages, $D_{\rm both}$ being the fraction of
comparisons that differ in both channels. Equation~\eqref{eq:channel-identity} is an
inclusion--exclusion relation: the joint estimand combines the two channel
disagreements and their overlap. A near-equality of $D_{\rm ansatz}$ and
$D_{\rm seed}$ can therefore arise from opposing channel-level contrasts
together with the overlap term, and all three contributions have to be read off
before the joint value is interpreted.

For the continuous responses we use a per-channel pairwise dispersion
$S^{(q)}$, structurally parallel to the label estimand. Writing $R^{(q)}_{acb}$
for channel $q$ of the response and $f_q$ for that channel's floor,
%
\begin{equation}
S^{(q)}_{\rm ansatz} =
\Big\langle \big[(R^{(q)}_{acb}-R^{(q)}_{a'cb})/f_q\big]^2
\Big\rangle^{1/2}_{a<a',\,c,\,b},
\label{eq:S}
\end{equation}
%
and $S^{(q)}_{\rm seed}$ likewise over block pairs at fixed ansatz. The
pairwise form is used because
$\sum_{i<j}(x_i-x_j)^2 = m\sum_i(x_i-\bar{x})^2$ holds for any finite set of
$m$ values, so the mean pairwise squared difference is exactly twice the
sample variance with the $(m-1)$ divisor and the $4$-versus-$5$ group-size
asymmetry cancels. $S^{(q)}$ is therefore exactly $\sqrt{2}$ times a sample
standard deviation -- an algebraic identity, not a distributional
assumption. $S^{(q)}$ is a dispersion of pairwise differences and carries no
information about whether the underlying responses clear their floors.

\subsection{Candidate selection and transport}

Operating-point candidates are selected on \emph{seed stability alone}, at the
(ansatz, condition) level: a pair is a candidate if the condition is labelled
robust under that ansatz in all three discovery blocks. Cross-ansatz agreement
is never a selection criterion, so the table is non-circular with respect to
the comparison it illustrates. It is not statistically independent of it: the
same response data enter both. Blocks 1--3 are the
discovery set and blocks 4--5 the holdout. Every condition in the discovery
union $U^{\rm disc}$ is then evaluated on the holdout blocks under \emph{all
four} ans\"atze, giving a within-ansatz replication count and a transport count
over $0\ldots4$ ans\"atze. Because $U^{\rm disc}$ is a set and its membership
changes with ensemble size, transport is reported as counts and never as a
rate. The same screen run at $n_{\rm real}=100$ is an \emph{extended
confirmation}, not a second holdout: the $n_{\rm real}=100$ aggregate contains
the $n_{\rm real}=30$ realizations as a prefix and reuses blocks 4 and 5, which
were already seen at the smaller ensemble.

\subsection{Registration and the ranking diagnostic}

The estimands, the candidate-selection rule and the checkpoints
$n_{\rm real}=5,10,20,30$ with primary endpoint $n_{\rm real}=30$ were fixed in
writing before any of these data existed. The extension to $n_{\rm real}=100$,
including its checkpoints, its hard stop and its reporting language, was
registered after the $n_{\rm real}=30$ result and before the extension data
were generated. The channel decomposition of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:channel-identity}, the family breakdowns and the ranking
diagnostic below were added after seeing results and are marked post-hoc where
they appear.

The ranking diagnostic is a complementary magnitude-ordering summary: the
pairwise Spearman rank correlation of $|\mathcal{R}|$ across conditions. It
weights the two channels differently from the classifier, since the released
magnitude is unnormalized while the classifier judges each channel against its
own floor. We test that difference directly by recomputing the correlation
under floor-normalized and single-channel magnitudes, and under the signed
$\Delta\chi_\phi$.

Nothing here is a population-level statistical claim. There are five
registered seed blocks; grid cells are paired by common random numbers across
geometry, case and ansatz, and are not independent replicates, so they are
never resampled as i.i.d. We report no confidence intervals and no
significance tests, and every threshold-dependent statement is scoped to these
operational floors.

The distinction between the ranking and the classification is central to our
conclusions and is illustrated in Fig.~\ref{fig:quadrant}: two ans\"atze may
order a set of conditions by response \emph{magnitude} almost identically while
still assigning many conditions to different categories. Ranking captures how
large the response is; the thresholded label records which response signs are
resolved above the floors. Within that label set, the four two-channel quadrants
capture the sign pattern of the baseline-relative responses, one ingredient of
a co-design decision. A final performance decision would also require the
absolute full-model leakage and dephasing proxies, which we do not optimize
here.

\paragraph{Unmatched seeds exaggerated the archived model disagreement.}
The archived analysis drew a separate noise realization for each ansatz, so a
cross-ansatz difference there carried a sampling difference as well. Repeating
that comparison with the seeding corrected, at matched ensemble size
$n_{\rm real}=5$, matched seed block and matched convention, raises the mean
classification agreement from $35.96\%$ to $56.94\%$ and the mean rank
correlation from $0.837$ to $0.917$. The correction removes $20.99$ of the
archived $64.04$ percentage points of disagreement, about one third; $43.06$
points survive matched seeding. It accounts for most of the \emph{revision} --
$20.99$ of the $24.60$ points by which agreement rises from the archived value
to the corrected $n_{\rm real}=30$ result -- and the archived numbers are
retained only as a provenance record.

\paragraph{Cross-ansatz disagreement is large under matched realizations.}
At the pre-registered endpoint $n_{\rm real}=30$, cross-ansatz disagreement is
$D_{\rm ansatz}=0.394$ and same-ansatz cross-seed disagreement is
$D_{\rm seed}=0.389$; the two are comparable there, and both are large. Averaged
over conditions, ansatz pairs and seed blocks, about $39\%$ of matched
comparisons assign different design-facing labels. Neither scale dominates at
that endpoint,
which is a comparison outcome and not a null result.

The aggregate hides a persistent pair structure. Across all eight registered
checkpoints the six ansatz pairs fall into three tiers: A/B$_z$ alone at the
bottom ($0.161$--$0.193$), the three pairs containing B$_x$ in a narrow middle
band ($0.352$--$0.400$), and A/A$_{\rm pocket}$ together with
A$_{\rm pocket}$/B$_z$ at the top ($0.478$--$0.532$). The third pair containing
A$_{\rm pocket}$, A$_{\rm pocket}$/B$_x$, sits in the middle band. Between $n_{\rm real}=30$ and $100$ no pair moves by
more than $2.6$ points while the seed-pair mean falls from $0.389$ to $0.302$.
The tiers name three specified diagnostic contrasts: among the contrasts
examined, the specified pocket localization produces the largest classification
change and the specified hot-spot weighting the smallest. The four ans\"atze form a set of specified
contrasts, so this names three of them and not a general ranking of modelling
axes. A and B$_z$ remain distinct: they are the most similar pair in the family
and still disagree on $18.3\%$ of conditions.

Uniform dominance does not hold. The two pairwise distributions overlap at
every checkpoint: at $n_{\rm real}=100$ one seed pair, blocks 3 and 5 at
$0.171$, falls below the lowest ansatz pair at $0.183$. As a post-hoc descriptive summary of the same
sixty (ansatz-pair, seed-pair) comparisons, 48 favour the ansatz side there,
against 34 at $n_{\rm real}=30$. Shared inactivity contributes to both sides and is
reported, not conditioned away: $11$ of $108$ conditions are below
threshold under every ansatz and block at $n_{\rm real}=100$, and shared
below-threshold assignments supply $45\%$ of ansatz-pair agreement. Restricting
to comparisons in which at least one member carries a two-channel quadrant
label, as a sensitivity analysis, leaves the contrast intact and larger,
$0.523$ against $0.326$.

\paragraph{The two scales behave differently in ensemble size.}
Over the eight registered checkpoints $D_{\rm seed}$ decreases from $0.540$ to
$0.302$ with no rebound, while $D_{\rm ansatz}$ stays within a $2.1$-point band,
$0.375$ to $0.396$, with no trend [Fig.~\ref{fig:overview}(d)]. The ordering
reverses between $n_{\rm real}=20$ and $30$, and the separation widens
thereafter, $R_D$ running from $0.73$ to $1.31$. Cross-ansatz disagreement thus
persists across the ensemble sizes and registered seed blocks examined, whereas
cross-seed disagreement decreases over the registered trajectory.

Two limits on reading this. $D_{\rm seed}$ was still falling at the final
checkpoint and the extension is hard-stopped there, so no asymptotic or
convergence statement is available. And $D_{\rm ansatz}$ is itself computed from
finite samples and retains an ansatz$\times$realization interaction, so its
flatness is an empirical property of this design, not a property of the model
family. At the registered checkpoints up to
$n_{\rm real}=30$ the two are of comparable size, so a cross-ansatz
disagreement reported without the cross-seed benchmark cannot be judged against
the label instability a change of random sample produces on its own.

\paragraph{The balance is observable dependent.}
The nine-category label is an exact bijection of the two channels' three-state
descriptions, so Eq.~\eqref{eq:channel-identity} holds identically
and the joint estimand is an inclusion--exclusion combination of the two
channel disagreements and their overlap [Fig.~\ref{fig:atlas}(a)]. At $n_{\rm real}=30$ the valley channel runs toward
sampling, $0.255$ against $0.362$, and the dephasing channel toward model form,
$0.190$ against $0.061$. The two channel contrasts are opposed, $-0.106$ and
$+0.129$, and the overlap term completes the identity at $-0.018$, leaving the
joint difference at $+0.005$. The near-equality is the arithmetic outcome of
three terms, not one channel prevailing. At
$n_{\rm real}=100$ the valley channel is approximately balanced, ratio $0.94$,
while the dephasing ratio rises to $4.75$.

The continuous responses agree, and must be stated separately. For
$\Delta\chi_\phi$ the cross-ansatz dispersion stays between $41$ and $54$ in
floor units over the whole trajectory and moves from $45.3$ to $44.1$ between
the two endpoints, while the cross-seed dispersion halves, $22.3$ to $10.2$. For
$\Delta P_v$ both fall steeply -- cross-ansatz $30.6$ to $24.4$ against
cross-seed $38.3$ to $20.1$ -- so no ensemble-size-stable cross-ansatz
continuous scale is isolated in that channel, and its ratio only reaches
$1.22$. Neither dispersion is monotone in $n_{\rm real}$: the seed dispersion
rebounds at $n_{\rm real}=60$ for $\Delta P_v$ and at $10$ for
$\Delta\chi_\phi$. $D_{\rm seed}$ is the only quantity here that is monotone
over all eight registered checkpoints. Which observable a design criterion
consumes therefore changes which uncertainty is larger.

\paragraph{Ranking and classification are decoupled across the family.}
This diagnostic and the comparisons in it were added after the registered
results were seen; both are post-hoc.
Rank correlation is high and narrow across the family, mean $0.913$ at
$n_{\rm real}=100$ with all six pairs between $0.844$ and $0.978$, while
classification agreement spans $0.483$ to $0.817$. The two do not track each
other [Fig.~\ref{fig:atlas}(c)]: A/A$_{\rm pocket}$ and A/B$_z$ differ by
$0.003$ in rank correlation and by $33.3$ percentage points in classification
agreement. Chance correction does not rescue the agreement -- Cohen's $\kappa$
runs from $0.338$ to $0.777$, and the two lowest values belong to
A/A$_{\rm pocket}$ and A$_{\rm pocket}$/B$_z$.

The rank metric weights the two channels differently from the classifier, and
that was the leading alternative explanation. Recomputing the correlation under
a floor-normalized magnitude moves the A/A$_{\rm pocket}$ against A/B$_z$ gap
only from $0.003$ to $0.015$ at $n_{\rm real}=100$, and single-channel
magnitudes move it less; channel weighting does not account for the decoupling.
Among the ranked quantities tested here, the one that tracks classification
agreement is the \emph{signed} dephasing response: its A/A$_{\rm pocket}$ against A/B$_z$ gap is
$0.154$, and its six-pair Spearman correlation with classification agreement is
$0.880$ at $n_{\rm real}=100$ and $0.771$ at $30$, against $-0.030$ and
$-0.029$ for the released magnitude. This implicates spin-response sign
information as a leading contributor to the decoupling. It is a post-hoc metric
over six non-independent pairs -- A/A$_{\rm pocket}$ and A/B$_z$ share A -- and
the classifier uses floor crossing as well as sign, so it is not a demonstrated
cause.

\paragraph{Seed-stable candidates transport unevenly.}
Selecting on seed stability alone over the three discovery blocks yields 24
(ansatz, condition) candidate pairs at $n_{\rm real}=30$, spread 7/4/6/7 over
A, A$_{\rm pocket}$, B$_z$ and B$_x$, and a discovery union of ten conditions
(Table~\ref{tab:retest}). Evaluated on the two holdout blocks, 20 of the 24
replicate within their own ansatz: an operating point that is stable across
seeds under one coupling profile generally stays stable under that profile.

Transport across the family is a different matter [Fig.~\ref{fig:atlas}(b)]. Of
the ten conditions, one is confirmed under no ansatz, two under one, three under
two, one under three, and three under all four. The registered
$n_{\rm real}=100$ companion analysis gives 31 pairs, a union of 13, within-ansatz
replication 24 of 31, and transport 2/3/3/2/3. This is an extended confirmation and not an
independent replication: the $n_{\rm real}=100$ aggregate contains the
$n_{\rm real}=30$ realizations as a prefix and reuses the same holdout blocks.
Of the 24 pairs, 22 are retained, 2 lost and 9 gained, and the two unions are
nested. The same three conditions receive
four-of-four confirmation at both prefixes. They are grid-local candidates
conditional on this diagnostic family, and three conditions do not support a
claim about which geometries are robust.


\section{Discussion}

Design-facing conclusions in this setting depend on the assumed coupling
profile, and the dependence is large under matched realizations. The
nine-category thresholded classification is what changes; its four two-channel
quadrants encode the decision-facing sign pattern that a co-design choice
consumes. Whether a given operating point is
labelled robust is contingent on which profile was assumed: three of the ten
seed-stable candidates are confirmed under all four ans\"atze, one under none.

The comparison against cross-seed disagreement is what makes this readable.
Showing that four different kernels give four different numbers is true by
construction. The informative content is how the two scales behave. Cross-ansatz
disagreement stays near $0.39$ across a twentyfold change in ensemble size,
while cross-seed disagreement falls by nearly half. At the smaller registered
checkpoints the two are of comparable size, so an observed ansatz sensitivity
cannot be judged large without the benchmark alongside it, and the judgement
changes with the ensemble size at which it is made. It also settles what the earlier archived
analysis was measuring: correcting its seeding removes about a third of the
disagreement it reported, and the rest survives matched realizations.

Three features of the disagreement structure carry more information than the
aggregate. First, ranking and classification come apart: two ansatz pairs
differing by $0.003$ in rank correlation differ by $33$ percentage points in
classification agreement, so a family can agree on which conditions respond
strongly while disagreeing on which of them improve. The fragile quantity is the
classification --- the one a design decision consumes. Second, the hierarchy is
observable-dependent. Spin dephasing shows a large cross-ansatz excess at both
endpoints; valley excitation runs toward sampling at $n_{\rm real}=30$ and is
approximately balanced at $100$. Which loss channel a criterion weights
therefore changes which uncertainty is larger. Third, the pairwise disagreements
separate into three persistent tiers across all eight checkpoints, so the
aggregate conceals a persistent structure among the specified contrasts. These
are three diagnostic contrasts within one family, not a factorial design, and
they do not identify a general physical axis of disagreement.

The value of the exercise is conditional on a specific fact: no generally
validated effective local spin--valley kernel is available for the shuttled-dot
and micromagnet regime considered here. A validated microscopic kernel would replace the
present diagnostic family as the preferred physical description and would
substantially reduce the specific model-form ambiguity studied here. Residual
parameter and structural uncertainty could still be assessed with the same
comparison. Design decisions today are made
under non-unique phenomenological proxies, so the disagreement between those
proxies is a real component of design risk, and a single proxy cannot expose it
however realistic it looks. Microscopically informed valley landscapes, such as
those constrained by alloy-disorder calculations and conveyor-mode
measurements~\cite{paquelet_wuetz_atomic_2022,losert_practical_2023,volmer_mapping_2024},
can enter the same comparison, and a microscopic coupling can be added as a
further member once one is validated for this setting. Whether that narrows the
spread is an empirical question.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
All conclusions are scoped to four diagnostic coupling profiles, one fixed
minimal spin--valley operator, one fixed charge-noise description, and the
periodic micromagnet reference geometry examined here. The four ans\"atze differ
only in their scalar spatial profiles, so operator-level alternatives are
outside the present scope. The same $1/f$ spectrum and a single representative,
non-device-calibrated displacement-noise amplitude enter both responses, since
$\Delta P_v$ measures the effect of charge noise on valley leakage and
$\Delta\chi_\phi$ the effect of the valley sector on charge-noise dephasing.
What is quantified is therefore coupling-profile uncertainty conditional on that
noise description; noise-spectrum and amplitude uncertainty are a separate axis
and are not scanned.

The thresholded labels are operational classifications under the stated
effect-size floors, not confidence-certified assignments, and every
threshold-dependent statement is scoped to those floors. Sensitivity to shared below-threshold
assignments is examined in the Supplementary Material. Nothing reported here is a
population-level statistical claim: there are five registered seed blocks, grid
cells are paired by common random numbers and are not independent replicates, and
no confidence intervals or significance tests are computed.

The registered trajectory stops at $n_{\rm real}=100$, where cross-seed
disagreement was still falling, so no asymptotic or convergence statement is
available. Cross-ansatz disagreement is itself computed from finite samples and
retains an ansatz$\times$realization interaction; its stability across the
trajectory is an empirical property of this design. The $n_{\rm real}=100$
candidate layer is an extended confirmation on blocks already seen at
$n_{\rm real}=30$, not an independent replication. The three conditions confirmed
under all four ans\"atze are grid-local and conditional on this diagnostic
family; three conditions do not support a claim about which geometries are
robust.

Natural extensions are to measured or finite-device micromagnet fields, to
families of noise descriptions, and to microscopically derived couplings and
operator structures. We leave these to future work because the purpose here is
to isolate coupling-profile dependence against a fixed geometric backdrop.

\paragraph{Implications for co-design.}
Two practical implications follow. First, a claim that a particular operating
point is robust should state which coupling profile produced it, and should
report whether the point transports across a family. Proposed mitigations such
as varying the shuttling velocity, dot shape, or
path~\cite{losert_strategies_2024} deserve the same treatment. Second, a cross-ansatz comparison should
carry a same-ansatz cross-seed benchmark at the same ensemble size, since at the
smaller checkpoints examined here the two are of comparable size.

A geometry or trajectory optimum obtained under one assumed coupling profile
remains model-conditional. Two routes strengthen it: constraining the effective
coupling independently, against microscopic theory or experiment, or
demonstrating that the optimum survives across a justified model family. The
comparison reported here supplies the second route's measurement, and says
nothing about which route is cheaper.

\section{Conclusion}

We have compared cross-ansatz and cross-seed variation in a design-facing
classification for spin shuttling through a periodic micromagnet field, under
common random numbers and across a registered range of Monte-Carlo ensemble
sizes. Two results are methodological. First, drawing separate noise
realizations per model mixes model differences with realization differences: in
the archived analysis, correcting the seeding removes about a third of the
disagreement it reported, and two thirds survive. In the finite-ensemble paired comparison used here,
matched realizations are what isolate ansatz dependence from realization
differences; without them an observed cross-ansatz difference mixes the two. Second, the two scales behave differently in
ensemble size, so a cross-ansatz disagreement is interpretable only against a
cross-seed benchmark measured at the same ensemble size.

The scientific result is that cross-ansatz disagreement in this setting is
large, persistent across the registered ensemble sizes and seed blocks, and
unevenly distributed. It concentrates in the thresholded classification while
magnitude ranking stays high; its excess over cross-seed disagreement is much
larger in the dephasing channel than in valley excitation; and it separates the
specified contrasts into three persistent tiers. Operating points selected for seed stability replicate within
their own ansatz and transport unevenly across the family. Design conclusions
drawn from a single phenomenological coupling profile therefore remain
model-conditional.

\paragraph{Future work.}
The natural next step is a microscopic effective $H_{sv}$ for this regime,
derived from orbital admixture, valley--orbit interaction, and $g$-factor
anisotropy~\cite{friesen_theory_2010,hosseinkhani_theory_2022,zhang_controlling_2021}.
Related gradient, strain, and spin--orbit design questions in Ge hole-spin
platforms~\cite{sarkar_effect_2025,mauro_strain_2025,geyer_anisotropic_2024,hendrickx_sweet-spot_2024,jin_probing_2024}
can inform the same program. Because the framework treats the coupling profile
as an interchangeable module, a microscopic model enters as an additional member
and is compared pairwise against the diagnostic family. Residual uncertainty in
microscopic input parameters will likely leave a finite degree of model
dependence, which the same comparison would measure. A factorial design over
gradient component and spatial localization, which the four profiles here cannot
support, would test whether the disagreement has an identifiable physical axis.
